# Atmospheric_muons
The Pierre Auger Observatory, located in Malargue, Argentina, is the world's largest experiment dedicated to detecting extreme energy cosmic rays. More than fifteen years after it began operating, knowledge of the high-energy universe has expanded significantly. 


More than fifteen years after it began operating, knowledge of the high-energy universe has expanded significantly. In order to answer the new questions that have arisen in the meantime, a second generation of measurements is underway, based on
improvements in the observatory's detection capacity, either by upgrading the
existing detectors or by adding new ones.
One of the mysteries that has recently emerged concerns the measurement of an excess,
compared to theoretical predictions, in the number of muons in showers originating from
ultra-energetic cosmic rays. This result suggests modifications in the theoretical description of
particle showers and has implications for our knowledge of the nature of primary particles
. Knowledge of the energy spectrum of muons is an important step
towards resolving this issue.
This project aims to study the feasibility of measuring the energy spectrum
of muons in the Auger context, based on computer simulations of particle flows and
interactions and the development of data analysis. This project
is part of the research activities of the LIP group in the Pierre
Auger Collaboration.
